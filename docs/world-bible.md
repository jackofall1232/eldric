# World bible — Millhaven

Original lore, characters, locations, the Blackwater Bridge story and its branching decision.

**The ten-chapter world map — where this region sits, what comes after it, and how the map grows —
is `world-map.md`.** This file covers chapter one only.

## The oath

The Broken King bound this land with oaths: real, binding, enforceable things. He broke, and the
oaths outlived him. Nobody alive remembers how to release one. Chapter one is the smallest version
of that story — one oath, one man who paid for it, and a player who has to decide whether keeping
a promise is worth what it costs.

## Millhaven

A river village of four buildings and about a hundred people, on the Blackwater upstream of the
bridge. It survives on the mill, the eastern fields and the road tolls, and it is one bad harvest
from not surviving.

- **Elara Vale** — Corven's sister. Wants the truth, not comfort, and says so.
- **Warden Rowan** — keeps the road. Calls it a beast because a beast is a thing you can kill,
  and because he has spare chains with fresh nicks in them.
- **Mara Fen** — hedge-wise, at the village edge. Knows the river remembers older bargains than
  Rowan does, and points at the saint in the north wood.
- **Corven Vale** — went to the bridge three nights ago. Is under it. Is singing.

## Places

**Whisperwood** (north) — the weathered saint, whose stone eyes point southeast at a trail of pale
mushrooms and the hidden glade beyond. **Blackwater Road** (east) — the abandoned wagon, clawed
from the inside. **Blackwater Bridge** — bare human footprints enter the river; webbed tracks
leave it. **The Sunken Ruin** — the Broken King's Watch, a reliquary, and the Thornhart guarding a
rusted river key. **The Gloam Cave** — the sealed gate, the three stones (river, crown, root), and
the Drowned Oath beneath.

## The decision

Break the iron seal and free Corven — the flood takes the eastern fields, and Millhaven will argue
about it for a generation. Or renew the binding — the harvest stands, the river runs quiet, and
every night the village pretends not to hear singing under the bridge.

Neither is correct. Both are remembered: in the Chronicle, in the tavern rumor, and in what Elara,
Rowan and Mara say to you afterwards.
